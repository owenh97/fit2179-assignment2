The Anatomy of Wealth

How National Factors Shape Billionaires in Malaysia, Australia and the World

Where Are the Billionaires?

Billionaires are not evenly distributed across the globe. The United States and China alone account for nearly half of the world's billionaires. But what does it take for a smaller nation like Malaysia or Australia to produce one?

Malaysia has just 19 billionaires compared to Australia's 47 — despite both being upper-middle to high-income economies in the Asia-Pacific region.

Which Industries Produce Billionaires?

Australia's wealth is rooted in the ground — Metals & Mining leads with 10 billionaires, reflecting the country's resource-rich economy.

Malaysia tells a different story: Finance & Investments ties with Real Estate at 5 each, reflecting a banking and property-driven economy. Notably, Malaysia has zero technology billionaires.

Does National Wealth Matter?

GDP alone does not determine how many billionaires a country produces. Singapore, with a much smaller economy than Malaysia, has produced 46 billionaires — more than four times Malaysia's count.

Malaysia punches below its weight. Despite decades of economic growth, it remains an outlier when compared to peers of similar GDP size.

How Old Are They?

Malaysian billionaires are overwhelmingly older — the median age is 71, and the youngest is 45. Wealth in Malaysia has not yet passed to the next generation.

Australia shows far more generational spread, with billionaires as young as 35. Melanie Perkins (Canva) and Mike Cannon-Brookes (Atlassian) represent a new wave of technology-driven wealth.

Who Are They?

Billionaires in both countries are overwhelmingly male. Malaysia's three female billionaires are all daughters of the same man — Teh Hong Piow, founder of Public Bank. Remove them, and Malaysia has no female billionaires at all.

Malaysia's self-made rate of 55% is notably lower than Australia's 67%, suggesting inherited wealth plays a larger role in Malaysian billionaire culture.
