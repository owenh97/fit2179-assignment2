The Anatomy of Wealth

How National Factors Shape Billionaires in Malaysia, Australia and the World

Section 01 · Global

Where Are the Billionaires?

Billionaires are not evenly distributed across the globe. The United States and China alone account for nearly half of the world's billionaires. Malaysia has just 11 compared to Australia's 43 — despite both being upper-middle to high-income economies in the Asia-Pacific region.

Section 02 · Malaysia

Malaysia's Billionaire Landscape

Malaysia's 19 billionaires are concentrated in the Klang Valley — Kuala Lumpur and Selangor — where banking, property and diversified conglomerates dominate. Wealth is geographically as concentrated as it is economically.

The majority of Malaysian billionaires operate out of Kuala Lumpur and Selangor. Robert Kuok, Malaysia's wealthiest at $12.1B, built his fortune through sugar, shipping and hospitality across Southeast Asia.

Unlike Australia, where billionaires span six states, Malaysia's wealth is heavily centralised in the western corridor of Peninsular Malaysia.

Section 03 · Australia

Australia's Billionaire Landscape

Australia's 47 billionaires are spread across its states — from mining magnates in Western Australia to tech founders in New South Wales and Victoria. Geography reflects industry: resources in the west, technology and finance in the east.

Western Australia is home to Gina Rinehart and Andrew Forrest — both mining billionaires whose fortunes rival the GDP of small nations.

New South Wales and Victoria house Mike Cannon-Brookes, Scott Farquhar and Melanie Perkins — all under 50, representing a generational shift in how Australian wealth is created.

Section 03.5 · Change Over Time

Who Gained? Who Lost?

Between 2023 and 2025, Malaysia saw the most dramatic proportional rise — from 11 to 19 billionaires, a jump of 73%. Australia grew modestly from 43 to 47.

China and the United Kingdom both lost billionaires over this period, while Russia, Canada and Germany saw significant gains. The global billionaire map is shifting.

Section 04 · Industry

Which Industries Produce Billionaires?

Australia's wealth is grounded on its soil. Metals & Mining leads with 10 billionaires, reflecting the country's resource-rich economy.

Malaysia tells a different story: Finance & Investments and Real Estate each hold 5, reflecting a banking and property-driven economy. Notably, Malaysia has zero technology billionaires.

The heatmap shows the same data from a different angle — darker cells indicate more billionaires in that industry. Australia dominates across most industries while Malaysia's billionaire base is concentrated in just a few sectors.

Section 05 · National Factors

Does National Wealth Matter?

GDP alone does not determine how many billionaires a country produces. Singapore, with a much smaller economy than Malaysia, houses 46 billionaires — more than four times Malaysia's count.

Despite decades of economic growth, Malaysia remains an outlier when compared to peers of similar GDP size.

Singapore is an outlier, with 8 billionaires per million people despite a moderate tax rate. Open trade policies and financial infrastructure matter more than tax rates alone. Malaysia's low education enrollment of 45% compared to Australia's 113% may partly explain the gap in billionaire production.

Section 06 · Demographics

How Old Are They?

Malaysian billionaires are much older — the median age is 71, and the youngest is 45. Wealth in Malaysia has not yet passed to the next generation.

Australia shows far more generational spread, with billionaires as young as 35. Melanie Perkins (Canva) and Mike Cannon-Brookes (Atlassian) represent a new wave of technology-driven wealth.

Section 07 · Gender & Origin

Who Are They?

Billionaires in both countries are overwhelmingly male. Malaysia's three female billionaires are all daughters of the same man — Teh Hong Piow, founder of Public Bank. Remove them, and Malaysia has no female billionaires at all.

Malaysia's self-made rate of 55% is notably lower than Australia's 67%, suggesting inherited wealth plays a larger role in Malaysian billionaire culture.

Section 08 · Wealth Depth

How Much Do They Actually Have?

The vast majority of billionaires in both countries sit in the $1–2B bracket. Being a billionaire does not mean being equally wealthy — most are closer to the floor than the ceiling.
